Already reports score thresholds chosen for 80%/90% eQTL sign accuracy and retained coverage. This forced the headline change from generic abstention to external task/context transportability.
2. **Atlas launch manuscript — occupied components.** Official preprint released with Atlas on 2026-09-08. Covers generic AVI benchmarking, rare-disease prioritization, rare-variant aggregation, saturation-screen comparisons, motifs and interpretation. No peer-reviewed version was located by the cutoff.
3. **RNU4-2 focused benchmark — nearby, not duplicative.** NaS Research, 2026-09-09, compares 435 transcript SNVs with AVI and reports weak rank correlation. It is a small locus-specific score benchmark, not five-cell reliability transport or budget utility.
4. **Severe-COVID A549 STARR-seq — nearby, not duplicative.** Weykopf et al., *PLOS Genetics* (published 2026-07-17), evaluates AlphaGenome against a disease-specific lung-cell reporter screen and reports substantial false negatives. It does not provide a general five-cell selective-risk or experiment-budget analysis.
5. **Siraj corpus — independent target publication.** Siraj et al., *Nature* (online 2026-02-25), supplies the five-cell assay data. It evaluates regulatory annotations and Enformer but does not contain AlphaGenome/Atlas in the article text located by search.
6. **Generic sequence-model uncertainty exists.** Bajwa et al., PMLR 240 (2024), studies uncertainty for genomic sequence-to-activity models. Therefore ensemble/discordance itself is not a first; the claim must remain Atlas-specific external transport and decision utility.
7. **Multi-ancestry model evaluation exists.** A 2026 preprint evaluates sequence-to-function models including AlphaGenome using multi-ancestry blood eQTLs. A broad ancestry benchmark is not available whitespace.
8. **Model-informed fine-mapping exists.** A 2026 preprint evaluates practical sequence-to-omics utility for fine-mapping reproducibility. The proposed Aim 2 remains distinct only because its endpoint is observed MPRA return per assay slot and locus diversity.
9. **Haplotype evaluation exists.** HaploPerturb, arXiv:2608.05002 (August 2026), includes AlphaGenome at Alzheimer’s loci. A haplotype-only AlphaGenome paper is not prioritized.
10. **AlphaGenome-guided wet-lab validation exists.** Zheng et al. (PubMed 41676477, 2026) reports an RHD regulatory-variant study with wet-lab validation. Do not claim first wet-lab validation.

**Corrections to the available claim:**

1. The original AlphaGenome paper already evaluates CAGI5 MPRA with zero-shot cell-matched DNase and learned DNase/RNA/histone combinations. A claim of first MPRA validation, first cell-matched analysis, or first multimodal evaluation is false.
2. The Atlas preprint already benchmarks AVI against ten held-out saturation-genome-editing screens and reports non-coding prioritization applications. A claim that AVI has not been experimentally benchmarked is false.
3. Weykopf et al. already evaluate AlphaGenome raw and quantile scores in A549 STARR-seq. They report 166 active variants, 29 allele-specific variants, AUCs of 0.68--0.75 for AlphaGenome features, useful directionality among a detected subset, and substantial false negatives. This directly occupies a single-cell reporter-assay validation and qualitative interpretability claim.
4. Nadal Vidal already reports cross-locus AlphaGenome score miscalibration on 15 Kircher MPRA saturation loci. A generic external-calibration or raw-score-comparability claim is occupied.
5. No located work jointly tests the independent Siraj five-cell corpus, exact-cell DNase/H3K27ac policies, global AVI and PIP baselines, fixed experimental budgets, and locus-diversity constraints. Searches included exact title/DOI combinations, `AlphaGenome Siraj`, `AlphaGenome five-cell MPRA`, `AlphaGenome AVI MPRA prioritization`, and `AlphaGenome DNase H3K27ac concordance`.

**Remaining claim, conditional on confirmation:** matched cell-context molecular scores may provide materially different experimental-prioritization utility from global AVI, and cross-modal agreement may act as a coarse reliability marker without improving the highest-confidence ranking policy. The contribution is a five-context decision audit under external assay shift, not a new predictor, a first validation, or a universal calibration result.
